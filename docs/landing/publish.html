🔥 Publish your private service to the world

Run it wherever you already have it running — a laptop, a Raspberry Pi, a spare VM, a container, your own AI agent — and make it reachable at a real HTTPS address, without opening a port, without a public IP, and without anyone (including us) seeing the payload.

Nothing here needs to run on our infrastructure. You bring the hardware and the idea; the tunnel does the rest.

Get your first success in 4 steps

  1. Register — get a username and password

    One-time, human step: create an account at the customer portal. This is the identity your subdomain and your published pipelines/agents will be tied to.

    Zum Kundenportal — Registrieren →

  2. Download the starter template

    A minimal, working "hello world" workflow pipeline — one role, one handler script that echoes your input back. It proves the whole loop (identity, discovery, the encrypted round-trip) with the smallest possible example, so you have a known-good starting point before you swap in your own idea. 

    git clone https://github.com/scimbe/CADS-Tunnel
cd CADS-Tunnel/examples/hello-world-pipeline
cat README.md   # the exact commands, copy-paste ready
    View the template on GitHub →

    You don't have to write the integration yourself. Paste this into Claude Code (or hand it to any coding agent) and it will read the docs, wire up your identity, and adapt the template to your idea:

   docs/agent-onboarding.md and examples/hello-world-pipeline/README.md.
2. Build ct-agent (there's a Docker one-liner in docs/install.md — I don't
   have a Rust toolchain installed).
3. Help me turn examples/hello-world-pipeline/hello-handler.sh into a
   handler for my idea: <describe what you want your service to do here>.
   It's currently running on: <your PC / Raspberry Pi / container / agent>.
4. Walk me through minting my channel identity, running the handler as a
   live role, and publishing my pipeline.

I already have an account at https://bunsenbrenner.org/portal
(username: <your username>).

  4. Your subdomain

    In the Standard tier you don't pick your own subdomain name — it's assigned automatically from your project id and your account's unique user id (e.g. hello-world-a3f9.bunsenbrenner.org), so names never collide and every subdomain traces back to an account. This is the current policy; a future tier may offer custom/vanity subdomains.

Security model, plainly stated: the tunnel exposes exactly one thing — the service you point it at (the handler you configured, on whatever port/process you chose). It does not expose, scan, or secure the rest of your machine, and we cannot see what runs on it. We provide secure (end-to-end encrypted) transport to your exposed service, not protection of the device it runs on. Keeping your own machine and the code behind your exposed service secure — including not introducing exploitable weaknesses through how you implement it — is your responsibility as the operator of that service; see Nutzungsbedingungen §3–§5.

What is "Bunsenbrenner"?

Picture a lab bench. A Bunsen burner isn't the experiment — it's the reliable, unglamorous heat source every experiment on that bench depends on, so you can focus on the idea instead of re-inventing how to make fire.

bunsenbrenner.org is that bench. It's the shared lab where you can test an idea, run it on whatever hardware you already have, and hand the result to other people — over a real, encrypted, publicly reachable address — without having to build or trust a whole platform first. Some ideas here started as a weekend demo and are now real, working pipelines with their own community of contributors. That's the point: a place to try things, and a straight line from "it works on my machine" to "here, try it."
